# petrknoll26

One page: an infinite canvas of images. Astro 7, Tailwind CSS v4, GSAP (its ticker drives the single animation loop).

- **Intro:** opening the canvas, the camera is already flying down a tunnel of lines in the colours of the work as "The office of Petr Knoll" and then the controls rise in at the centre and leave again ([`introtitle.ts`](src/canvas/introtitle.ts)): the info bar's, or on a touch screen only its pictograms. Then the flight speeds up into a wormhole, and each tile comes in as a ray for every cell of its colour grid, closing up into a pixelated image that the real one takes over from and glides in the rest of the way ([`intro.ts`](src/canvas/intro.ts)). About 5.6 s, the launch and landing the last 2 of them, most of that easing out, and up to 1.5 s more if the images on screen haven't loaded, on every fresh load of `/`. Any click, scroll or key cuts it short and counts as usual. It doesn't play coming back with the browser's buttons, on the about view, with reduced motion, or without a fast WebGL 2.
- **Pan:** drag, scroll (trackpad in any direction), or arrow keys (Shift for a bigger step).
- **Zoom:** two states, out and in. Pinch, ctrl/⌘ + scroll or `+` zooms in on the image under the cursor; pinching back, `−` or `0` zooms out. `Space` or `Enter` zooms in on the active image (the one in the focus frame), and pressing it again (or `Esc`) zooms back out. On a narrow screen, as on a phone, each image zooms to fill 90% of the width (or 62% of the height, if that's less, to leave room for its caption), the lens letting go as it comes up, and paging eases between zooms. Zoomed in, the view pages between neighbouring images: each scroll, swipe or drag moves one image in its direction (diagonals included), always settling with that image exactly centred, and every other image fades.
- **Click an image** to open the focused view on it (it zooms in, centred). Zoomed in, clicking another glides it to the centre.
- **Project view:** in the focused view, `P` narrows the canvas to the focused image's project ([`project.ts`](src/canvas/project.ts), drawn by the renderer). The camera draws back from the focused image, and the cells nearest it take the rest of the project for a while, each image gliding in across the canvas from where its nearest copy really is (or from a screen-width off in its direction, if none is within 8 cells); everything else fades away, the furthest first, so the canvas recedes onto the project. What is left is a small canvas of the project alone, the same tiles on the same lattice, fitted to the window (down to 0.4×, further back than the canvas's own zoomed-out view, for a big project). Landed, it is browsed like the canvas, dragged and scrolled, though let go with any of it off screen it glides back until all of it is in view, and its images trail about a third as much as on the canvas. Each image that comes in takes over its nearest copy's own tile, image and all, so nothing is seen to change hands: the copy simply sets off, and it hands the tile back on the way out. Leaving sends each image back where it came from as the camera returns from wherever it has got to: `P` or `Esc` to exactly the focused view it was opened from; focusing one of its images (a click, `Space`, zooming in) onto that one, the camera following it back to where it lives on the canvas and landing in the focused view on it. One progress drives it all, camera included: a critically damped spring chasing an eased goal, so it sets off gently, settles in about 0.9 s, and turns smoothly mid-way, keeping its momentum. While it moves, videos hold still and tiles that would be too faint to see aren't made at all, so it runs at the display's full rate; the canvas takes no input until it lands. With reduced motion nothing moves: it only fades.
- **Project label:** the active image's project name sits in a small translucent pill just above the top-left corner of the focus frame ([`projectlabel.ts`](src/canvas/projectlabel.ts)), zoomed out and in (where it takes the place of a title above the caption). It lives inside the frame, so it hands over from image to image with it and hides when it does, and a new project's name eases in.
- **Caption:** zoomed in, the focused image's category with the alt text just below sits centred under it ([`caption.ts`](src/canvas/caption.ts)); above it, the project label names its project. They're anchored to the focus frame (CSS anchor positioning). It shows only while the view rests on an image (zoomed in, landed, nothing moving, the frame done handing over), rising in slowly line by line (0.9 s each, 0.1 s apart). The moment anything moves, a drag, a scroll, a key, a zoom or a flight, it is pinned where it stands and leaves fast (0.14 s), so it never rides along with the frame; it comes back once all is still. Title and category come from the image's Markdown entry, along with the alt text the tiles already use. Without anchor positioning it sits in the bottom left corner instead.
- **About / Back:** a plain-text link at the top right, set like the wordmark ([`Header.astro`](src/components/Header.astro)). About (click or Enter, zoomed out or in) flies the camera on into the canvas, leaving `/about`: under the same header, where the link reads Back, the about text rises in, a narrow column centred in the window. Back, `Esc` or the browser's back button flies the canvas back in to the exact view you left, zoomed out or in; forward flies out again. Modified clicks open the link in a new tab as usual.
- **Info bar:** the controls at a glance, fixed at the bottom right ([`InfoBar.astro`](src/components/InfoBar.astro), from the Figma design, its icons in [`src/assets/infobar`](src/assets/infobar)): Drag/Pinch/Scroll, Navigate (arrows, Space, Esc), Project (`P`, the project view), Theme (`L`) and About (`A`, which does what the header link does). Drawn at 0.85 of the design's size (`SCALE`, applied with CSS `zoom` so it all shrinks in proportion) and set like the header (white, difference blend, no pointer events, on a 36 px band 16 px up from the bottom and 24 px in), and only where there is a fine pointer and at least 760 px of width.
- **Theme:** light or dark, as the system is set, until `L` switches to the other one (on the canvas or the about view). Its two colour tokens are registered as colours (`@property`), so a switch fades them in CSS on their own: nothing is snapshotted or paused, and a drag, scroll, zoom or flight runs on through it. The choice is kept in `localStorage` and set by an inline script in [`Layout.astro`](src/layouts/Layout.astro) before the first paint, so a reload never flashes the other theme; `data-theme` on `<html>` overrides the system's `prefers-color-scheme` in [`global.css`](src/styles/global.css).
- **Wordmark:** "Petr Knoll" at the top centre, a size smaller under 760 px wide. It is white with a difference blend, so it reads on either theme and over images. It ignores the pointer, so the canvas drags and scrolls under it.
- **Type:** Timeless Sans is the site font (Tailwind's `--font-sans`) and Timeless Serif is there as `font-serif`, each in every weight with italics, from `src/assets/fonts/timeless`, self-hosted through Astro's Fonts API (`astro.config.mjs`) with a metric-matched fallback. A browser only downloads the weights the page uses; only Sans Medium, the wordmark's weight, is preloaded. Their licence (`LICENSE.pdf` beside them) allows serving them on the site but not putting them in a public repository, so this repository must stay private.

Needs Node ≥ 22.22 (`.nvmrc` pins 24).

## How it works

All tunable numbers are in [`src/canvas/config.ts`](src/canvas/config.ts).

- **The world is a pure function** ([`world.ts`](src/canvas/world.ts)). `cell(i, j)` gives a cell's image, shape, offset and z-order from an integer hash of its coordinates. Nothing is stored and nothing is "respawned". Pan away and back and the same images are in the same places.
- **No repeats, by construction.** Each cell belongs to one of 6×5 lattice classes, and each class draws from its own slice of the images. Two cells can only show the same image if they are at least 6 columns or 5 rows apart. [`layout.ts`](src/canvas/layout.ts) sizes the images so that span never fits on screen (up to 3440 px wide). With the lens on, its edge ring is the one exception; see Lens below.
- **Overlap budget.** The horizontal jitter is derived from a 15% cap on how much of the smaller image an overlap may cover. Columns are brick-offset, so overlaps always read as layered, never as one merged image. Images in the same column never overlap.
- **Physics** ([`physics.ts`](src/canvas/physics.ts)).
  - Fixed 240 Hz step, with inputs applied at their event timestamps, so the same gesture gives the same motion at 60, 120 or 144 Hz.
  - Wheel and trackpad move a target that the camera follows with an 80 ms time constant (the slight drag). The OS already supplies trackpad momentum, so nothing adds more.
  - Dragging follows the pointer 1:1. On release the canvas glides with a 300 ms velocity half-life.
  - Zoom eases toward a target with a 90 ms time constant, keeping the world point under the cursor fixed at every step.
  - Zoomed in, clicking an image glides the camera until that image's grid centre is in the middle of the screen (zoomed out, a click zooms into it instead) ([`focus.ts`](src/canvas/focus.ts)). Glides (this one and paging) ease out: an ease-out cubic that leaves at once and slows smoothly to rest. They take 0.45–0.9 s, longer for longer moves. A glide that starts from a flick keeps the flick's speed and arrives sooner rather than overshooting. Any pan, drag or zoom takes over mid-glide.
- **Zoom states** ([`main.ts`](src/canvas/main.ts), [`physics.ts`](src/canvas/physics.ts)).
  - Out is zoom 1. In is fixed per viewport: a portrait image's long edge fills 70% of the screen's short side, but never so far that images go soft (`zoomInLevel` in [`layout.ts`](src/canvas/layout.ts)).
  - One pinch or ctrl/⌘ + scroll gesture switches state once it has zoomed 12% one way; more in the same direction does nothing, and swinging back the other way switches back.
  - Zooming in grows the image under the cursor and glides it to the centre in one move (physics `ZOOM_TO`) over 0.7 s, eased by `ZOOM_EASE`, cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1): it sets off gently (no jolt on the first frame), is quickly up to speed and spends the rest slowing smoothly into place. Zooming out keeps whatever is centred in place. Input during either move takes over the panning, but the zoom still finishes at its level.
  - Zooming onto another image than the framed one, the focus frame steps aside: it fades out in 0.12 s, moves to the new image only once out of sight, and fades back in round it with the caption.
  - Zoomed in, the physics pages (`setPaging`): the view is anchored on the centred image, and one gesture moves at most one image, to the neighbour nearest one pitch away in its direction.
    - Pulling stiffens and can never get further than 90% of a pitch from the anchor.
    - A scroll commits the moment it has pulled 18% (it has no release to wait for: trackpad momentum keeps it going for a second or more), glides to the neighbour, and ignores the rest of itself.
    - A new trackpad swipe arrives in the same event stream as the last one's momentum. Momentum only ever slows down, so the scroll's speed (averaged over ~50 ms, each event weighted by the time it covers, so merged or unevenly timed events don't fake a change) slowing to half its peak and then speeding up again (to twice its slowest plus 150 px/s, or back to the peak) is a new swipe. It is credited with everything it has moved since that slowest point.
    - A drag decides on release: past 18% (counting 0.15 s of release velocity, so a quick flick counts) it moves on, otherwise it springs back, starting at the release speed either way.
    - Arrow keys page one image per press; clicking an image goes straight to it.
  - Zoomed in, every image except the active one fades to 15% opacity, easing as the active image changes. The lens and the image trail work as they do zoomed out.
- **Image trail** ([`trail.ts`](src/canvas/trail.ts)). Every image hangs on its grid position from its own damped spring with air drag, simulated on the same 240 Hz step.
  - While the canvas moves, each image trails behind by lag × velocity along the velocity vector.
  - Lag tapers along the direction of motion, from 8 ms at the viewport edge the images move toward to 32 ms at the opposite edge, plus a small per-image weight. The sheet stretches out behind the gesture like cloth pulled from its front edge.
  - When the direction changes, each image's lag eases to its new value.
  - When the canvas stops, images settle back (with a slight overshoot) and snap exactly onto the grid.
  - Zooming moves images radially, so the taper runs from the zoom anchor outward instead. Reduced motion turns the trail off.
- **Lens** ([`lens.ts`](src/canvas/lens.ts)), like the watchOS app grid. `?flat` turns it off.
  - Images inside the middle ~45% of the screen are full size. Toward every edge they shrink smoothly to 45%, smallest in the corners, in a rounded-rectangle (squircle) shape.
  - The grid is pulled toward the centre by the integral of that scale, so spacing tightens as fast as the images shrink. Each image's scale is taken at its corner farthest from the centre, so the lens can't push images into each other. The most it adds is a sliver, under 2% of an image.
  - Drag, pinch and zoom map the pointer back through the lens, so they act on the image actually under it.
  - The edge ring squeezes in more of the canvas, so `LENS_REPEAT_SHARE` sets how much of it the no-repeat sizing counts. At 1440×900: 1 means no repeats ever (18 images on screen); the default 0.55 gives 22 images, with a repeat between two small edge images on about 3% of screens; 0 gives 30 images, with repeats on 75%.
- **Active image** ([`renderer.ts`](src/canvas/renderer.ts)). After every frame the renderer picks the image whose drawn centre (lens and trail included) is nearest the viewport centre, and stacks it over every other tile. It only switches once another image is closer by `ACTIVE_SWITCH_MARGIN` (12 px), so two near-equal neighbours never flicker. `renderer.activeImage()` returns it, and `onActiveChange` reports each switch.
- **Intro** ([`intro.ts`](src/canvas/intro.ts), drawn by [`introlayer.ts`](src/canvas/introlayer.ts)). A pinhole camera: a tile at depth 1 + `z` lands exactly where it lies on the canvas as `z` reaches 0, and everything is drawn as a box from where it is back to where it was a moment ago, which is its motion blur. A zoom is seen in ratios, so the easing is on the logarithm of the depth: a hard launch, a peak a fifth of the way in, and a long ease-out. Before it, the camera cruises at a steady rate that runs on into the launch, so the flight only ever speeds up. The lines have real places along the tunnel, so the camera truly flies past them, and the launch is the same flight, faster. Each image's manifest entry holds its colour grid, 24 × 18 cells of average colour (21 × 21 if square) as hex digits (`pixels`, from [`scripts/pixels`](scripts/pixels); `npm run pixels:build` refreshes them). Each cell is a ray that lands on that cell, at its own pace: most run ahead of the camera, a few hang back and tear through to catch up, and tiles arrive in waves, and lines in the same colours, as wide and as dense as the rays, fill the way from where the camera sets out to the tiles, with no gap at the centre; the ones nearest the tiles keep outside the view, to be off screen when they land. Depth fog tapers everything smoothly with distance, so the far end of the tunnel recedes rather than ending. The rays themselves stay hidden until the controls have gone, and come out of the vanishing point as the flight speeds up. It all starts from the grids in the page, so it needs no image and waits on no network. A tile shows its grid, pixelated, until its image is in, on the canvas as well as here, so nothing changes at the handover. One instanced draw on a temporary WebGL 2 canvas over the tiles (no dependency, about 25,000 instances, pixels capped at 1.5×). The time goes on loading. The real tiles lie underneath, the middle of the view loading first (`fetchpriority`, which holds for the canvas as a whole), and once the images on screen are in, the videos on screen are fetched, unless the browser asks to save data, so they are moving on arrival. If the images aren't in by the launch the cruise runs on until they are, for up to 1.5 s more. Once the rays have closed up the canvas fades to them and is removed, and a single transform on the tile layer glides them in the rest of the way. The header and info bar wait on `data-intro`, set in [`Layout.astro`](src/layouts/Layout.astro) before the first paint. `?test` leaves the intro out unless `?intro` asks for it.
- **The about view** ([`flight.ts`](src/canvas/flight.ts), routing in [`main.ts`](src/canvas/main.ts), [`Site.astro`](src/components/Site.astro)). `/` and `/about` are two views of one document: both pages render the same site, and going between them is a flight of the canvas, never a page load.
  - The route lives in the URL (History API, any query carried over). About pushes `/about`; Back goes back in history when you came from the canvas (so Forward returns), or pushes `/` when `/about` was opened directly. The browser's back and forward buttons run the same flights, and a change of mind mid-flight turns it round from wherever it has got to.
  - Leaving, the camera holds where it is drawn and input locks. The caption sends its lines out and the focus frame fades; the header stays, its link label sliding from About to Back through its mask.
  - Each on-screen image gets a depth for the flight only (1 to 3, from a hash of its cell; browsing stays flat), and the camera dollies forward through a pinhole projection: an image is drawn depth / (depth − travel) times bigger, moving outward from the screen centre by the same factor. Nearer images grow and leave first. Each blurs (up to 16 px, like leaving the focal plane) and fades out as it nears the camera, gone at 80% of the way to it, and the camera travels until the farthest has faded, over 0.8 s, eased by `FLIGHT_EASE`, cubic-bezier(0.5, 0, 0.25, 1): setting off gently, rushing, and easing off as the last images go. Only the header is left, and the about text, which has come towards the camera from behind the canvas.
  - Coming back, the flight runs in reverse over 0.75 s, the images arriving gently from the camera, sharpening, and settling softly into place, to exactly the view it left: same camera, zoom state and focused image. It then settles into the zoom state if the flight interrupted it.
  - The about text is Markdown, [`src/content/about.md`](src/content/about.md), set centred in a 34 rem column: a statement (`##`, large, in the serif and 65% wider than the column; the wordmark is the page's `h1`), a line under it, then chapters over 16 px text. Each chapter's `###` is a row: "Chapter", its numeral dead centre in the statement's serif and size (`<span class="numeral">`), and its title (`<span class="title">`). Where the text has `<!-- services -->` (before the chapters), the services listed in its frontmatter, unlabelled on the page (a region named Services): a column per group (its name over its items), wrapping on narrow screens. After the chapters, the awards from its frontmatter: a small Awards title over the list, set in the serif; then the social links, listed the same way. Taller than the window, it scrolls with GSAP's ScrollSmoother: the page itself scrolls (only on the about view; the canvas never does), and the text eases after it, settling about a second later. Touch scrolls natively, and with reduced motion it follows at once (`smooth` 0.001: at 0 ScrollSmoother stops moving the text). It fades out under the header. It is a layer behind the canvas, a far plane one depth unit beyond where the camera stops (`flightFar` in [`flight.ts`](src/canvas/flight.ts)): flying in, it comes towards the camera through the images, growing by perspective from about 30% to its own size, about the viewport's centre however far the text is scrolled, fading in from a third of the way and sharpening from an 8 px blur as it reaches the focal plane; flying back, it recedes and fades the same way while the images return in front of it. Whichever of the canvas and the text isn't showing is inert, so neither takes the other's clicks or scrolls; on the about view the wheel is the browser's, so the page scrolls and ScrollSmoother moves the text.
  - The view is also kept in sessionStorage (its centre in units of S, and the zoom state), so opening or reloading `/about`, or a reloaded tab going back to `/`, starts flown past from that view and flies back into it.
  - With reduced motion nothing moves: the images fade out over 0.3 s.
- **Focus frame** ([`focusframe.ts`](src/canvas/focusframe.ts), `.focus-frame` in [`global.css`](src/styles/global.css)). A thin ink-coloured outline (a 1px inset box shadow) `FOCUS_FRAME_PADDING` (8 px) outside the active image, with its corners following the image's. It is placed with CSS anchor positioning: the tile it is leaving is anchor `--focus-from`, the one it is moving to is `--focus-to`, and each inset is a blend of the two anchors' edges by `--focus-mix`, a registered custom property that transitions from 0 to 1 over `FOCUS_FRAME_DURATION` (0.45 s). Both anchors stay live, so the frame stays locked to the images while the canvas pans, zooms, lenses and trails; only the hand-over animates. If the focus moves on mid-hand-over, the next one starts from where the frame is drawn. No script runs per frame. With reduced motion it jumps, and browsers without anchor positioning don't show it.
- **Rendering** ([`renderer.ts`](src/canvas/renderer.ts)).
  - A pool of tiles covers the cells around the viewport, with extra margin in the direction of travel.
  - Each tile is placed in screen space as (world − camera) × zoom + its trail offset, so the numbers written to CSS stay small however far you travel.
  - Each tile is laid out (sized in CSS, so rasterised, with an image variant to fit) at its own zoom and scaled from there. Tiles are laid out again once the zoom settles, or drifts by 1.4×, but never while a zoom-to is in flight: re-laying out every tile mid-zoom costs frames and swaps images for sharper ones mid-move. Instead the image being zoomed into is laid out for where it lands before it sets off, so it is sharp all the way (drawn smaller until it arrives) and its full-size variant loads on the way. The e2e test checks every frame of a zoom in and out: no long frames, a gentle start, no lurch, nothing re-laid out mid-zoom.
  - Each tile loads AVIF/WebP from a `srcset`, shows its dominant colour while loading, and fades in once decoded.

## Image content

Each image's text lives in a Markdown file in [`src/content/images/`](src/content/images/), a content collection ([`src/content.config.ts`](src/content.config.ts)). There is no editor or admin: edit the files and commit them.

```md
---
file: "01a1066c-6146-7010-bfa2-519a378861b9.jpg"
title: "Mango can mockup"
category: "Development"
alt: "Silver cans printed with an orange mango script, stacked on a diagonal…"
caption: "Early mockup used to test the script at shelf distance."
notes: "Third-party mockup template; don’t publish."
---

Optional write-up, in Markdown.
```

- **The file name is the image's id** (lowercase words joined by hyphens). Projects refer to images by id, so re-cropping or replacing an image file only changes `file`.
- **Fields.** `file` is the image's (or video's) name in its manifest. `title` (up to 80 characters), `category` and `alt` are required. `caption` (up to 160) and `notes` are optional. `alt` (up to 200) says what the image shows. `notes` are private and the body is a write-up; neither is shipped to the page.
- **Categories** are `Brand` (product and campaign photography), `Logo` (the mark or wordmark is the subject), `Website` (layouts for screens: heroes, banners, product grids) and `Development` (mockups, renders, spec views, packshots). They're listed in [`src/canvas/images.ts`](src/canvas/images.ts).
- **Validated** when the page builds. The collection schema checks each file, and the error names the file and the field. [`resolveImages`](src/canvas/images.ts) then fails the build if a manifest image has no entry, an entry names a file that isn't in the manifest, or two entries describe the same image.
- **Shipped:** only `alt` reaches the page. It is in the page data and set on every tile. Everything else is available at build time through `getCollection('images')` for whatever UI needs it.

Adding an image: add it to the manifest, then create `src/content/images/<id>.md` with its `file`, `title`, `category` and `alt`, and list the id in a project. A video is added the same way, with its `.mp4` as `file` (see Videos).

The current 96 entries are samples: the alt text describes each image and video, but titles, captions, notes and write-ups are invented.

## Projects

Projects group images: every image belongs to exactly one project, and the images in a project are its associations. Nothing in the UI uses them yet.

- **Authored** in [`src/content/projects.json`](src/content/projects.json), a content collection ([`src/content.config.ts`](src/content.config.ts)). Each project has an `id`, `title`, optional `year`, and `images`: image ids (entry file names in `src/content/images/`), cover first.
- **Validated** when the page builds. The collection schema requires at least 3 images per project. [`resolveProjects`](src/canvas/projects.ts) then fails the build if an id repeats, an image id doesn't exist, or an image is in two projects or none.
- **Shipped** to the canvas in the page data as `projects`, with images as indices into `images`. `createProjectIndex` turns that into lookups: an image's project, its siblings, a project by id.

The current 17 projects are samples with invented names, but for the last six: Off Menu (most of the videos and the work without a project of its own), AtomsAI, Tenacity Capital, Ditto, Explorations and Bettermint. They group images by dominant colour into runs of 3–9, so each looks like one body of work. Replace them by hand with real projects; re-run `npm run placeholders:projects` only while they're still samples.

## Work images

`src/assets/work/` holds real work: images exported from Figma, each with a line in its `manifest.json`. [`src/assets/media.ts`](src/assets/media.ts) lists them after the placeholder images.

- **Built** by `npm run work:build -- <folder>` ([`scripts/work/build-work.mjs`](scripts/work/build-work.mjs)) from every PNG, JPEG or WebP in the folder. Draw the artboards at a tile shape (4:3, 3:4 or 1:1; anything else is cropped to the nearest, about its centre unless the script's `ANCHOR` says otherwise) and export them large (the case-study images are about 2000 px; the Figma ones are exported at 4× (the most Figma allows), 3200–6400 px on the long edge). Each becomes a lossless WebP that keeps its colour profile (Figma exports Display P3). The build replaces the folder.
- **Served** like every other image: the page builds AVIF and WebP variants from the master at each standard size (`VARIANT_SHORT_EDGES` in `config.ts`, 256 to 2880 px on the short edge) and converts them to sRGB. Unlike photos (AVIF 55, WebP 76), work is encoded at AVIF 75 and WebP 90, since compression shows around fine type and flat colour (`detail` in `media.ts`). The focused view on a 2× screen loads the 1024 px variant, about 40 KB; the largest variants, 2880 px, come to 140–730 KB and load only when a tile is drawn that large.

The current 17 are AtomsAI (a billboard, the wordmark and the mark's construction), Spring (habit charts), GridBank (two sets of app screens), Flex (a poster photo, cropped from 3:2 at its left edge), Tenacity Capital (three), Ditto (four), Resonant (two), the Tangent footer, 3D audio icons, Bettermint (three renders) and a Chill Pill vial. With the four newer videos, they replaced 27 placeholders, which `build-placeholders.mjs` now leaves out (`REPLACED`).

## Videos

`src/assets/videos/` holds the canvas videos, each with a poster frame and a line in its `manifest.json`. [`src/assets/media.ts`](src/assets/media.ts) puts them after the placeholder images in one list, the order the page and the content entries resolve.

- **Built** by `npm run videos:build -- <folder>` ([`scripts/videos/build-videos.mjs`](scripts/videos/build-videos.mjs)) from every `.mp4`, `.mov` or `.webm` in the folder. Each is cropped about its centre to the nearest of 4:3, 3:4 or 1:1 (or the one set for it in the script's `ASPECT`, where the nearest would cut the subject), scaled to at most 1080 px on its short edge, stripped of audio and encoded twice: VP9 WebM (constant quality, CRF 42: against the source it measures a higher SSIM than the MP4 and comes out 17–53% smaller) and H.264 MP4 with faststart, plus a 360 px pair of copies for tiles drawn small. The poster frame is taken a third of the way in, or where the script's `POSTER` says. The build replaces the folder.
- **On the canvas** a video's tile is an image tile built from its poster frame, with the same variants, placeholder colour and alt text, and the video plays over it, muted and looping ([`renderer.ts`](src/canvas/renderer.ts)): the WebM where the browser plays VP9, the MP4 where it doesn't (older Safari). It fades in once it is actually playing, so a tile never flashes black; once in, the poster and placeholder colour under it are cleared, or a different frame under it (a light poster under a dark scene) shows through the anti-aliased rounded edge as a light fringe.
- **Playing** only while its tile is on screen (an IntersectionObserver), and only on the canvas: on the about view, off screen or with reduced motion, it holds its frame. It loads nothing until it first plays (`preload="none"`). A tile laid out small (up to 450 device px on its short edge, `VIDEO_SMALL_EDGE`) plays the 360 px copy, so the zoomed-out canvas never decodes 1080p for a thumbnail; laid out larger, as the image zoomed into is, it swaps to the full copy at the same moment.

The current eleven are the Off Menu site, its menu and a reel of it, two interaction experiments, a can render, a Crocs product render, the Remi logo, the Tangent site and two Bettermint renders (a turning mint, a refill simulation). Their sources are about 77 MB; the WebM copies come to 5.7 MB (the MP4s 7.5 MB), the small ones another 1.5 MB (1.8 MB), and each loads only once its tile is on screen.

## Placeholder images

`src/assets/placeholders/` holds 34 images from the Cosmos collection [Social Tonic](https://www.cosmos.so/petrknoll/social-tonic): 29 images plus the poster frames of 5 videos. One 360×450 image was too small and was left out, 29 were replaced by work images and videos, and 28 more (five whole sample projects) were dropped to make room for the work.

Each image is cropped to whichever of 4:3, 3:4 or 1:1 loses least, and the shapes are then evened out where that costs at most 30% more of the picture. Most of the collection is 4:5 posts, so it ended up 15 landscape, 38 portrait and 38 square, and, less the 57 replaced or dropped, is now 4, 18 and 12.

The manifest records each image's crop, credit, Cosmos element URL and source file. Run `npm run placeholders:collect && npm run placeholders:build` after the collection changes. Every lattice class needs at least two images (the world test checks it): the canvas as a whole (placeholders, work and videos, 67 now) needs at least 60 for 6×5, a few more when one shape dominates. Below that, reduce `REPEAT_COLS`/`REPEAT_ROWS` in `config.ts`.
