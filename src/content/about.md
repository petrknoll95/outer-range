## Good design gives you something to feel, something to recognize, and very little to figure out.

Currently at Off Menu

<!-- services -->

### <span class="numeral">I</span> <span class="title">On Intent</span>

Knowing what the work needs to do gives you room to experiment. It helps you judge which ideas deserve pursuing and which are just interesting distractions. A familiar pattern should earn its place as much as an unusual one. With a clear direction, you can take a few risks and still recognize when you’ve wandered off course. Every decision needs a reason, even if that reason is simply to make someone feel something.

### <span class="numeral">II</span> <span class="title">On Philosophy</span>

Clarity can have character. A little tension, an unexpected proportion, or a sharp contrast can give the work something worth paying attention to. The balance is in making it distinctive without making people work to understand it. For a brand, that means a visual language with enough consistency to be recognizable and enough freedom to keep evolving. For an interface, it means finding something fresh that still feels natural in use.

### <span class="numeral">III</span> <span class="title">On Process</span>

Making is a way of thinking. Some answers only appear once there’s something to react to, so the process needs room to change direction. An idea that looks convincing in a sketch might fall apart in a browser. An experiment might reveal a better starting point. Then comes the refining, including the occasional small adjustment that takes an unreasonable amount of time. The challenge is keeping sight of the whole while giving the details their due.

### <span class="numeral">IV</span> <span class="title">On Ethics</span>

Design asks for people’s attention and influences their decisions. That comes with a responsibility to be clear about what’s being offered and honest about what happens next. The words on a button, the visibility of a choice, and the ease of leaving all deserve consideration. A useful test is whether a decision still feels fair when you’re on the receiving end. People should be able to understand what they’re agreeing to without having to become suspicious first.
